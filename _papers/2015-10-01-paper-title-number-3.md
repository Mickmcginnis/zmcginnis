This paper uses the [Robbins Lunar Crater database](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1029/2018JE005592) and the Monte Carlo method to analyze lunar crater sizes in the context of the [Late Heavy Bombardment Theory](https://astrobiology.nasa.gov/news/re-thinking-a-critical-period-in-earths-history/), a moment in astronomical history in which high collision rates amongst asteroids and planets occurred, driven by post accretion and planetary instability.
